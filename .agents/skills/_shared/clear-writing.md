# Clear writing for persisted Atlas prose

Use this contract while drafting or revising prose that Atlas will save. It does not replace the evidence, trust, review, or publication rules of the owning workflow.

- Start with the engineer's question or outcome. Explain plain meaning before Atlas terminology.
- Give each paragraph one job. Name the actor, action, and result; claim causality only when evidence supports it.
- Use exact Atlas terms consistently. Put rules beside the action they govern and move nonessential mechanics to advanced reference material.
- Repository prose explains source organisation, ownership, boundaries, and reading routes.
- Component prose explains an independently addressable runtime or reusable unit, including what it does, uses, and produces.
- Flow prose explains ordered steps, conditions, and handoffs. A step can be a component, infrastructure item, external system, manual action, or unresolved participant.
- Infrastructure prose explains a package or resource, why it matters, how it is used, and its operational boundary.

## Scan for page-specific meaning

Before a persistence preview or return, scan page-specific explanatory prose. Exclude reusable policy, templates, generated text, code, quotations, and parser-owned structures.

- If a sentence could appear unchanged in another project's record, replace it with an evidenced actor, mechanism, boundary, input, output, failure behaviour, or effect. If evidence cannot make it specific, remove it or preserve the gap.
- Cut filler and restatements of headings, fields, or nearby sentences.
- Replace inline labels that only introduce a restatement. Keep labels that identify distinct items and add information.
- Prefer direct verbs to `serves as`, `stands as`, `acts as`, and `provides a way to`.
- Name the actor when passive wording hides ownership or causality. Keep passive wording when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.

These checks are review prompts, not banned words or mechanical errors. Preserve governed terms such as `must`, `never`, and `only`.

Never change wording at the cost of precision. Preserve frontmatter, YAML, controlled values, stable IDs, lifecycle, confidence, coverage, evidence, technical names, commands, code, link targets, paths, generated markers, structured tables, parser-owned headings, safety language, unknowns, conflicts, exclusions, and the direct-versus-inferred distinction.

Do not hand-edit generated content or rewrite committed staging evidence. Factual corrections, connection changes, changed scope, and softened requirements belong in the owning evidence/curation workflow.

Before returning persisted prose, verify that the opening states why the page exists, the first screen needs no prior Atlas vocabulary, every explanatory sentence is record-specific or reusable by design, every material claim retains its support and meaning, no new certainty or relationship was introduced, protected structures remain intact, and unknowns remain visible.
